Problem Statement

Process mining tools — conformance checkers, deviation detectors, and OCEL-based analytics — have matured significantly over the past decade. Systems such as Celonis, ProM, and pm4py can reliably identify where a real process execution diverges from a reference model. But the overwhelming majority of deployed systems stop at the dashboard.

A conformance alert tells an analyst that PO-0217 is 11 days late and that three customer orders are at risk. It does not evaluate which alternative supplier has available capacity, draft the customer notification, or determine whether the exposure level warrants escalation to a procurement director. That work still happens in email threads, Slack messages, and manual ERP lookups — minutes or hours after the alert fired.

⚠ The gap between detecting a deviation and acting on it is where supply chain value leaks. Existing tools provide visibility; they do not provide execution.
Research Gap

Three intersecting literatures are relevant here, and none of them address the full problem:

Process Mining

Produces deviation signals from event logs. Does not reason about downstream impact or generate remediation actions.

LLM Agents

Capable of multi-step reasoning and action. Lack grounding in process context — no awareness of which process step failed or what the conformance trace looks like.

SCM Automation

ERP workflow automation handles routine cases. Breaks down on novel deviations that require contextual judgement rather than rule matching.

No existing system combines process-aware deviation detection with an autonomous reasoning layer that can evaluate severity, inspect downstream impact across an object-centric event log, select a mitigation strategy, and produce executable recommendations — while preserving human oversight for high-stakes decisions.

Contribution

This prototype demonstrates a Process-Aware Agentic Loop (PAALS) — a closed-loop system that bridges the gap between process mining outputs and operational execution in supply chain management.

The primary contributions are:

C1

A deviation-to-action pipeline that uses OCEL-structured event data to provide a LangGraph agent with the process context required to produce grounded, actionable recommendations rather than generic responses.

C2

A structured human-in-the-loop protocol with explicit approval thresholds based on financial exposure, customer criticality, and supplier strategic tier — preventing autonomous execution on high-stakes cases.

C3

A supply chain digital twin that models object relationships (Supplier → PO → Customer Order → Customer) as a directed graph, enabling deviation impact propagation analysis.

System Architecture

The system is structured as a seven-stage loop. Each stage has a defined input, output, and failure mode.

Stage 1
Deviation Event Detection
Process mining engine compares actual event log traces against the reference purchase-to-pay model. Conformance violations are emitted as typed deviation events with entity IDs, timestamps, and preliminary severity classification.
Stage 2
OCEL Context Retrieval
The deviation ID is used to retrieve the full object-centric event log context: linked PO, supplier profile, delivery record, and all downstream customer orders. This gives the agent process-aware grounding rather than just a text description.
Stage 3
LangGraph Agent — Risk Evaluation
The agent (deviation_agent.py) runs a compiled LangGraph state machine. Node evaluate_risk classifies operational risk using exposure, severity, supplier tier, and customer count. Node assess_downstream estimates production impact and SLA breach probability.
Stage 4
Action Strategy Selection
A deviation-type × risk-level action matrix maps each case to one of seven canonical actions: evaluate_alternatives, draft_customer_notice, escalate_to_procurement, reroute_shipment, hold_payment, trigger_quality_audit, emergency_order.
Stage 5
Communication Drafting & Alternative Identification
For customer-facing actions, the agent produces a structured draft notice. For sourcing actions, it queries the supplier capability graph and returns ranked alternatives with estimated cost premiums.
Stage 6
Human Approval Gate
Approval is required when: exposure > $150,000 · affected customers ≥ 2 · supplier strategic tier = 1 · risk level is high or critical. Below these thresholds, the recommendation can be auto-executed.
Stage 7
Execution & Resolution
On approval, the recommendation status transitions to approved and the deviation is marked resolved. A full audit trail of reasoning steps, approval decisions, and timestamps is persisted to SQLite.

Suppliers span 11 countries across East Asia, South Asia, Southeast Asia, Europe, North Africa, and North America, with realistic lead times (7–30 days), port origins, currencies, and strategic tier classifications. Purchase orders are generated over a 180-day window with probabilistic delivery outcomes. The live event feed simulates ongoing operational activity via WebSocket broadcast at random intervals.

Kaggle Dataset · DataCo Smart Supply Chain —

Alongside the synthetic seed, PAALS imports the public DataCo Smart Supply Chain dataset (CC BY 4.0). Each Market × Shipping Mode lane becomes a fulfilment supplier (DC-SUP-*) whose reliability is its measured on-time rate; every order becomes a PO, delivery and customer order; real late shipments and suspected-fraud orders become deviations the agent can act on.

Kaggle Dataset · USAID SCMS Shipments —

The Supply Chain Shipment Pricing dataset is USAID's delivery history for health commodities (HIV and malaria medicines and tests) bought for PEPFAR programmes. Its real vendors become suppliers (SC-SUP-*), recipient countries become customers, and each PO and shipment keeps its real scheduled and delivered dates. Late arrivals and goods receipts recorded more than 14 days after delivery become deviations.
